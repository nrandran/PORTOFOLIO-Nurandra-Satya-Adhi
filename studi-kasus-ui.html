UXUI/UX case study

One HRIS, two kinds of users: HR admins on the web and employees on their phones

At Toton Group I designed the interfaces of GAIA Core (employee data, SSO, access management) and TechTon PAS (attendance, leave, overtime, payslips), then built them myself. This page walks through the design decisions.

The problem

HR and management needs cover several things at once: employee data, access to many applications, attendance, leave and overtime with tiered approval, and payslips. The people using the system work in very different ways.

Admins and HR work on wide screens with lots of tables and settings. Employees open the system from their phone, briefly, for one purpose: clock in, request leave, or view a payslip.

The design challenge: keep a secure, feature-rich system feeling simple for both groups, and consistent across every application.

Process

  1. Requirements discussions. Research and conversations with the IT and HR managers about SSO, payroll and approval flows.
  2. Design in Figma. Flows and screens were laid out before any code was written.
  3. Implementation. Web with PHP MVC, mobile with Flutter, and every application signing in through a single account (SSO).
  4. Testing and documentation. Thorough black box testing and system documentation.
  5. Presentation and training. A presentation to upper management and training for HR users.

Design decisions

GAIA Core · Web admin

Access management that makes sense without technical training

Permission settings are usually complicated. At the top of the page I placed three short steps: create a role, set menu access, add members. The rules are written in everyday language, for example "no tick means the menu does not appear".

The role table shows the allowed applications as labels, plus the number of menus and users, so an admin can review a role without opening its details.

TechTon PAS · Employee web

A dashboard that answers "what do I need to do today?"

The top shows only a brief identity. Below it is Self Service: large icon cards for Attendance, Leave & Permission and Overtime, each in its own colour for quick recognition. The sidebar groups the menu into "Main Menu" and "Employee Portal".

The logo, sidebar, header and card style match GAIA, so people moving between applications through SSO never feel they have entered a different product.

TechTon PAS · Mobile

Main action on top, everything else one tap away

The blue Today's Attendance card shows clock-in and clock-out times at the very top, because that is what people check most. Company announcements are a card carousel, followed by a three-column grid of coloured icons with numeric badges for requests waiting for action.

The bottom navigation has only three tabs (Home, Attendance, Profile) so it stays within reach of one hand.

Security as part of the experience

Because the data is sensitive, I treated several security choices as UX decisions: biometric authentication in the mobile app, a PIN to open payslips, PDF payslips with a watermark, and access per menu managed from one place so people only see what is relevant to them.

Results and what I learned

The system runs at Toton Group and I have presented it to upper management. For the SMB-TGR website I optimized the landing page to a Google PageSpeed score of 100.

Because I design and build, every design decision is tested against implementation reality. What I want to go deeper on next is more structured usability testing with end users, and a documented design system.
